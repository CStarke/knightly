# Knightly Usability Test

## Introduction

Thanks for trying Knightly. Our app serves as one place where Calvin students can find the campus information they use every day. Imagine you’re a new student navigating campus. We have prepared some scenarios for you to try. The account and events in this session are for practice.

You’re helping us test the app. Take whatever approach feels natural. If something feels confusing, please tell us. You can skip a task or stop at any time. My teammate will take notes during the session. We’ll leave your name out of the report.

## Background Questions

- Think of the last time you needed information about campus. What were you looking for, and where did you look first?
- Have you tried something similar to Knightly before?
- Have you ever helped run a student club?

As you work, please think out loud. Tell me what you’re looking for and what you expect to happen. What questions do you have before we start?

## The Test

1. A friend mentioned the Calvin women’s golf match against Hope College. Find the announcement and tell them what day and time the match starts.
2. You spot an announcement for the women’s volleyball game against Otterbein and want to read it after lunch. Save it in Knightly and show me how you know it’s saved.
3. You’d also like to hear live music. Look across campus, including clubs you don’t follow, and find one music event you could attend. Who’s hosting it?
4. You’re about to get lunch. How many regular meal swipes do you have left? If you wanted to bring a friend, how many guest passes does your demo account show?
5. You’re deciding what to eat at Commons. Find today’s menu and tell me one thing you could get for lunch.
6. You left your physical Knight Card in your room. Show me the digital ID you’d present to the cashier.
7. Your friend texts, “Where should we meet for the volleyball game?” Bring back the announcement you saved and tell them where the game is being played.
8. Your friend has the details now. Remove the volleyball announcement from your saved posts. How can you tell it’s gone?
9. Your friend has never been to Van Noord Arena. Show them where it is on the campus map, make the building label easy to read, and then show the whole campus again.
10. You heard a thunderstorm is coming. Find the notice. When does the watch end, and when was the notice sent?
11. A new student asks how they could reach Campus Safety dispatch through Knightly. Point out where they would start. Stop before placing a call.
12. Knights Robotics posted about its autonomous rover trials. Find the club’s announcement. Where are the trials taking place, and what does the team plan to test?
13. Imagine you’re the new leader of Knights Robotics. Claim the club for your demo account using `9F2L-8P4X-6W`. What on the screen tells you the club is now yours to manage?
14. Your co-leader wants a follow-up event at the same location as the rover trials. Draft a post titled **Rover Trials Recap** that mentions the location and one thing the team planned to test. Set the event for December 10, 2026, from 1 to 3 p.m.
15. The club has no photo for the recap. Give it a banner using one of Knightly’s designs, then show what’s ready for your co-leader to review. Leave the post unpublished.

## Thank You

Thank you for spending part of your day with us and for talking us through your experience with Knightly. It takes a little extra effort to explain what you’re thinking while you’re trying to do something, and we really appreciate that. Since we built the app, we already know where to look. Something as simple as seeing where you first went to check your meal swipes helps us understand what makes sense to someone using Knightly for the first time.

Thank you for being honest with us and for helping us make the app easier for the next student who uses it.

### Facilitating the Test

Plan for a 30–45 minute session. Allow about 4 minutes for the opening, background questions, and first impression, 25 minutes for the fifteen tasks, and 2 minutes for feedback. Read each prompt separately.

Answer questions about the session before starting. During a task, give the participant time to explore. If they go quiet, ask “What are you looking for?” If they ask where to go, try “Where would you look if you were on your own?” Offer help or move on if they are stuck, and note what you said.
